First, in the Mail User Agents activity, I learned more about how email works. I learned that an email client is a program that lets you send and receive emails. I also learned about web-based email like Gmail and Outlook. One thing I found useful is that you can access web-based email from different computers as long as you have Internet access. I use email a lot for school, so this helped me understand better how the email services I use actually work. I can use this information in college and at work to keep my emails organized and make sure I can access important messages when I need them. Second, in the E-mail Encryption activity, I learned about ways to make emails and attachments more secure. I learned that MIME allows people to send different types of files, such as pictures, videos, and other documents, through email. I also learned about S/MIME, PGP, and GPG, which can help protect email messages. Before this lesson, I did not know much about how email encryption worked. I think this will be useful in the future because I may need to send important files or information for school or work. It is good to know that there are ways to help keep that information protected. Third, in the Creating and Sending E-mail Messages Using Thunderbird activity, I learned more about the different parts of an email. This included the To, Cc, Bcc, subject, message, attachments, and signature. I also learned the difference between Reply, Reply All, and Forward. I already used some of these features before, but I did not always know exactly what each one did. I can use this knowledge when emailing my teachers, applying for jobs, or communicating with people at work. It can also help me avoid sending an email to the wrong person or forgetting to include an attachment. Overall, this lesson helped me understand email better and showed me why it is important for school and work. I learned about email programs, security, and how to write and send emails correctly. These are simple skills, but I know I will use them a lot in the future.
